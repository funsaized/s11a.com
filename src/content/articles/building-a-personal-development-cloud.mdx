In the age of personalized software, I wanted a personal cloud. The idea came down to three things:

- I wanted agents, dev servers, and long-running jobs to keep working after I closed my laptop. My workspaces should 'feel' like they're on the internet.
- I had perfectly good compute sitting around (m1 mac mini, 2016 TB MBP, NZXT prebuild 3080TI) and wanted to use each machine for what it does best.
- I wanted moving between them to feel like a seamless development experience rather than a collection of boxes I had to babysit.

So I networked my machines together, gave their apps real names and trusted HTTPS, and made the whole setup easy for both me and my agents to reach.

And of utmost importance, was doing all of that without any of it being exposed to the public internet.

## My requirements for the design.

My acceptance criteria can basically be boiled down to a statement: I want a **secure**, always-on hub for AI agents. It should foster long running terminals, tests, CI, and local web apps. It's important to consider that I'll primarily be working from a laptop (my m4 mac mini) that sleeps and changes networks.

Additionally, I want native Linux for when macOS is the wrong kernel for the use case and to avoid the bloat that comes with turning my mini into a hypervisor.

As open and local models advance, I want local GPU inference that doesn't live on a public API.

For my web-facing projects, browser-trusted HTTPS and readable hostnames was priority. Insecure origin browsers are too much of a tax in 2026.

Lastly, and probably most importantly **ZERO PUBLIC INGRESS**. This includes port-forwarding and funnels.

## The three-layer mental model

The method to materializing ... is to split three jobs that people usually smash together:

| Job                                  | Layer                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Who is this device, and may it talk? | Tailscale (identity, WireGuard, policy)         |
| What is this app called?             | Public DNS pointing at a Tailscale address      |
| How does a browser reach it?         | Caddy on the Tailscale IP, proxying to loopback |
