I used to think good documentation was about being concise. Remove the fluff, get to the point, right?

Turns out I've been thinking about it backwards for years. The best documentation isn't written at all. It's empathized into existence. You have to feel what it's like to not know something before you can explain it.

Here's what changed how I think about writing technical docs.

The curse of knowledge

Once you understand something, you cannot remember what it was like to not understand it. Every abstraction you've internalized becomes invisible — and every doc you write silently assumes it.

The fix isn't more words. It's starting from the reader's question, not your answer.

"The best documentation isn't written. It's empathized into existence."

Problem first, solution second

Compare the two ways of describing the same API:

// what I used to write
"Returns a continuation token."

// what actually helps
"Ran out of time mid-query? Cosmos hands you a
 bookmark — pass it back to resume where you left off."

Same fact. One assumes the reader already knows why tokens exist; the other meets them at their problem. Questions are vulnerabilities — good docs make it safe to ask them.
